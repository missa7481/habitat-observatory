REPRESENTATION LEARNING

Compare ecological, pretrained and learned features

01

Ecology-informed covariates

Use 19 bioclimatic variables and the 41-feature environment recipe as interpretable baselines. Seasonality, rainfall lags, vegetation, surface water and urban context capture plausible mosquito ecology at their native scales.

Limits: global layers do not observe household containers or local control practices.
02

Pretrained Earth-observation embeddings

Compare the existing 64-dimensional annual AlphaEarth representation with ecological features. Frozen embeddings borrow from broad satellite pretraining; they are not independently interpretable environmental measurements.

Next candidates: SatMAE, AnySat, Prithvi-EO-2.0 and TerraMind released representations.
03

Linear and early fusion

Concatenate Environment41 and AlphaEarth64, then fit the established regularized GLM/RF baselines. A compact early-fusion MLP learns a nonlinear projection over the same 105 input features.

Scaling is fit on the training partition only; all six frozen outer spatial folds are retained.
04

Nonlinear late fusion

Train separate environment and embedding MLP experts. A small meta-MLP combines their spatially out-of-fold scores; inner scores use four grouped folds based on 5° cells.

This is a small-data ablation, not a new foundation model or evidence that more complex fusion will generalize better.

PAPERS THAT INFORM THE METHODS

Advanced representation learning and data fusion

Species-distribution modeling · preprint

OCELOT: Planetary-Scale Species Distribution Modeling with Satellite Foundation Embeddings

Combines coordinates and satellite embeddings with bias-aware cell sampling and shared taxonomic structure. A useful design precedent for relative occurrence—not a guarantee of gains at mosquito-data scale.

van Merriënboer et al. (2026), arXiv:2611.00284 ↗
Temporal and multispectral pretraining

SatMAE: Pre-training Transformers for Temporal and Multi-Spectral Satellite Imagery

Masked-autoencoder design for time-series and multispectral satellite structure; motivates testing frozen seasonal representations rather than pretraining on the small mosquito cohort.

Cong et al. (2022), NeurIPS ↗
Multimodal and scale-adaptive EO · preprint

AnySat: One Earth Observation Model for Many Resolutions, Scales, and Modalities

Self-supervised JEPA with scale-adaptive encoders across varied sensors. Relevant where satellite resolution and modality coverage vary geographically.

Astruc et al. (2024), arXiv:2412.14123 ↗
Multitemporal EO foundation model · preprint

Prithvi-EO-2.0: A Versatile Multi-Temporal Foundation Model for Earth Observation Applications

A large multispectral time-series model; practical for this project as a released frozen encoder or parameter-efficient adaptation, not from-scratch pretraining.

Szwarcman et al. (2024), arXiv:2412.02732 ↗
Generative multimodal EO · arXiv version

TerraMind: Large-Scale Generative Multimodality for Earth Observation

Studies multimodal fusion across geospatial modalities. Generated or imputed modalities should be treated as model outputs, not additional independent ecological evidence.

Jakubik et al. (2025), arXiv:2504.11171 ↗
Pseudo-absence design · peer-reviewed

On the selection and effectiveness of pseudo-absences for species distribution modeling with deep learning

Shows why background selection, imbalance and spatial evaluation are central design choices for deep SDMs; pseudo-absences remain design-dependent.

Zbinden et al. (2024), Ecological Informatics ↗
Deep SDM benchmark · peer-reviewed

The Performance and Potential of Deep Learning for Predicting Species Distributions

Large like-for-like comparison motivates conventional baselines, spatial holdouts and leakage checks before adding more complex networks.

Kellenberger et al. (2026), Global Ecology and Biogeography ↗
Mosquito ecology and remote sensing · peer-reviewed

Comparing Satellite and Ground-Based Measurements of Environmental Suitability for Vector Mosquitoes in an Urban Landscape

Direct *Aedes albopictus* evidence for comparing satellite-derived land/climate features with weather and in-situ microclimate; the study is regional, not a global transfer test.

McMahon et al. (2022), Journal of Medical Entomology ↗

Foundation-model papers establish representation-learning methods on Earth-observation tasks; they do not establish mosquito-distribution accuracy. Preprints are labeled as such. Released weights and datasets retain their own licenses.

LIMITATIONS AND NEXT STEPS

More observations do not automatically mean more labels.

Coverage gaps

Sampling remains clustered, and the 2026 independent survey evidence is U.S.-focused. Global maps in poorly sampled tropical and remote areas are extrapolations.

Observation semantics

GBIF, citizen-science claims, habitat reports and trap surveys have different error, effort and detection processes. They are kept in separate roles rather than pooled as interchangeable labels.

Abundance is deferred

Available counts differ by trap type, duration, season, life stage and spatial grain. No globally standardized abundance target is suitable for a continuous world map.

Next model milestone

First secure more independent tropical surveys, a DOI-frozen occurrence snapshot and fresh spatial validation. Only then consider mapping the new MLP or adapting additional pretrained encoders.
